# Primary-mark critique

Review basis: the approved warm-community direction, the project forbidden
list, and local renders at 16px, 32px, 48px, and full card size. Five internal
rounds were rejected before this set for drifting into propellers, flowers,
cards, bars, paws, or sports-ball geometry.

## Candidate A — Shared Ground — Grade: B

- **Concept fit:** Three visibly different energies lean into one warm base,
  making gathering and belonging immediate without drawing literal people.
- **Brand compliance:** It uses only the approved palette and no borrowed social
  brand asset; the varied proportions remove the explicit bar-chart reading,
  though a faint crowd-or-books ambiguity remains.
- **Craft:** The broad orange ground gives the black forms a confident shared
  silhouette, while the changing tilt keeps the composition socially loose.
- **Legibility:** The ground and three-part rhythm survive at 16px; the cream
  seams become clearer from 32px upward.
- **Ownability:** The warm ground can recur as a navigation underline, timeline
  carrier, or motion baseline across the product.

Verdict: The idea is right and warm; the remaining crowd/books ambiguity is an
execution-level issue that can be softened after selection.

## Candidate B — Common Table — Grade: C

- **Concept fit:** The shared table is the most explicit community metaphor,
  but the scattered outer forms need explanation before they read as a
  gathering.
- **Brand compliance:** The mark respects the one-accent palette and contains no
  copied reference motif, text, audio mark, heart, house, or moderation symbol.
- **Craft:** The asymmetry avoids the earlier paw-like arrangement, but the
  orange table visually floats over the forms instead of feeling structurally
  shared.
- **Legibility:** At 16px it reduces to a strong orange lozenge with three dark
  fragments, losing much of the table story.
- **Ownability:** The table could support campaign illustrations, but the icon
  itself is less distinctive than the other two.

Verdict: Warm in intent but structurally weaker; refinement would require a new
composition rather than a small execution pass.

## Candidate C — Neighborhood Patch — Grade: B

- **Concept fit:** Three unequal regions form one compact warm patch, expressing
  individuality inside a shared community without depicting a crowd.
- **Brand compliance:** It uses Hearth Orange as the common field, stays within
  approved neutrals, and avoids the reference brands and every forbidden
  category cliché.
- **Craft:** The integrated coral silhouette and irregular internal seams feel
  more authored and tactile than separate shapes placed around a center.
- **Legibility:** The outer patch and three-region structure remain recognizable
  at 16px, with the irregular seam geometry becoming the 512px reward.
- **Ownability:** The patch can become a flexible identity system for case cards,
  community moments, avatars, and motion without relying on a generic social
  icon.

Verdict: The strongest complete identity idea; its remaining seam-weight and
silhouette refinements are execution-level.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **C — Neighborhood Patch:** recommended; best combination of warmth,
   community, small-size recall, and system potential.
2. **A — Shared Ground:** clearest gathering gesture, slightly more literal.
3. **B — Common Table:** warm metaphor, weakest standalone symbol.

Choose C because its idea is both specific and extensible. A is a viable
alternative if immediate human gathering matters more than abstract ownability.
